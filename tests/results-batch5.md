### Your visa route (useful for every German application)
I checked this on the official Make it in Germany page (https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card):
- **EU Blue Card standard salary threshold (2026):** at least **€50,700** gross a year.
- **Reduced threshold, €45,934.20:** applies to shortage occupations (the list includes "Academic STEM professionals") and to **new entrants whose degree was obtained less than three years ago**. You need approval from the Federal Employment Agency (BA) to use it. Your MSc was 2023–2024, so you'd probably count as a new entrant until some point in 2027. That depends on the date on your certificate, so check it.
- **Your degree must be "comparable to a German academic qualification".** The page points to its Recognition section and anabin. Some employers also ask for this: Helmholtz Munich wants a "Statement of Comparability" for foreign degrees. If you don't have one, it's the most useful thing you can start now.
- **Job offer:** must last at least six months and must match your qualification.
- Technician jobs in German universities and institutes are usually paid on the TV-L or TVöD **E9a** grade. The adverts I saw didn't publish the euro figure. So for any technician job, ask for the gross annual salary before you count on a Blue Card. If it's below the threshold, ask the employer which other skilled-worker route they would support.

This isn't legal or immigration advice. For your own case, check the official German sources or ask a qualified adviser.
